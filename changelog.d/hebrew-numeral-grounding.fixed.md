Ground numerals that Hebrew statutory text prints with a maqaf or a fraction slash. Hebrew attaches a one-letter prefix preposition to a following numeral with a maqaf (U+05BE, the Hebrew hyphen), and `_clean_source_text_for_numeric_extraction` had no rule for it: the Income Tax Ordinance section 121 rate schedule's `mem`-maqaf-84,120 was misread as the trailing "120" and National Insurance Law section 68(b)'s `bet`-maqaf-2.24 was dropped outright, so the CI grounding gate rejected bracket bounds and multipliers that are printed in the statute verbatim. Detach the maqaf before a digit the way the cedi, naira, and kwacha prefixes are already detached. Separately, recognise the Unicode fraction slash (U+2044): Income Tax Ordinance section 36 prints a quarter credit point as `1⁄4`, which the precomposed vulgar-fraction table cannot see. Contribute the fraction and its printed numerator and denominator, unguarded — U+2044 exists only to typeset fractions, so unlike the ASCII slash it needs no context test. Israeli consolidations also flatten a marked-up mixed number into a digit run glued to the fraction (section 66(c)(4)(a)'s birth-year child credit prints as `21⁄2`, two and a half); the flattening is lossy, but only the mixed reading is a value the section states, so it claims the run and the improper reading of it (10.5) is suppressed. A fraction whose numerator or denominator is wider than the flattening produces (`21⁄100`) is still read as written.
